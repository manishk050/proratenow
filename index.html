How to Calculate Pro-Rated Rent

Pro-rated rent is a partial rent amount for a tenant who occupies a property for only part of a billing month. One common method is the actual calendar-day method: divide the monthly rent by the exact number of days in that month, then multiply by the number of rental days that fall inside the month.

Pro-Rated Rent = (Monthly Rent ÷ Calendar Days in Month) × Active Rental Days
  1. Choose the month being prorated.
  2. Count the actual calendar days in that month.
  3. Divide monthly rent by the month's day count to get the daily rate.
  4. Count the lease days that overlap the month. ProCalc includes both the start and end date.
  5. Multiply the daily rate by the active rental days.
Example: $3,000 rent in a 30-day monthIf the tenant is responsible for 12 days, the daily rate is $100.00 and the pro-rated rent is $1,200.00.

30-day method vs. actual-days method

Some leases or local practices use a fixed 30-day divisor instead of the exact number of days in the month. ProCalc uses the actual calendar-day method because it automatically reflects February, leap years, 30-day months, and 31-day months. Your lease or local rules may require a different method, so use the method stated in the agreement when one is specified.

How to Calculate Pro-Rated Salary

A pro-rated salary is a partial salary amount for an employee who works only part of a pay period. This calculator uses a calendar-day approach: monthly salary divided by the actual number of days in the target month, multiplied by the calendar days covered by the employment date range.

Pro-Rated Salary = (Monthly Salary ÷ Calendar Days in Month) × Active Calendar Days

When this method is useful

Joining mid-month, leaving mid-month, estimating a first or final paycheck, or checking a calendar-day payroll calculation.

When to verify another method

Some employers calculate partial salary from working days, hourly rates, fixed divisors, payroll calendars, or local labor rules. Confirm the employer's payroll policy before treating an estimate as final.

How ProCalc Handles Dates

The calculator first finds the exact first and last day of the target month. It then intersects that month with your lease or employment date range. Dates outside the target month are ignored, which means a range can safely span multiple months without overcounting.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does pro-rated rent mean?

It means charging rent only for the part of a rental period that applies to the tenant, rather than charging the full monthly amount.

How do I calculate rent for part of a month?

Using the actual-days method, divide monthly rent by the number of calendar days in the month and multiply that daily rate by the number of rental days you are responsible for.

Does ProCalc count the move-in day?

Yes. The calculator treats the start and end dates as inclusive when they overlap the selected target month.

How does ProCalc handle February and leap years?

It derives the month length from the calendar. February therefore has 28 days in a normal year and 29 days in a leap year, such as February 2028.

What if my lease starts in one month and ends in another?

Select the month you want to calculate. ProCalc counts only the days from your date range that overlap that target month.

Should pro-rated salary use calendar days or working days?

That depends on the employer's payroll policy and applicable rules. ProCalc uses calendar days. If your organization uses working days, hourly rates, or another divisor, use that policy instead.

Is this calculator legal or payroll advice?

No. ProCalc provides a mathematical estimate. Lease terms, payroll practices, taxes, employment rules, and local law can change the amount actually due.

Does ProCalc send my amounts or dates to a server?

The calculator itself runs in your browser. Your inputs are saved in localStorage so they can survive a refresh. The calculator does not require a backend to perform the calculation.
